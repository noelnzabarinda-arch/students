What is Chemistry?

Chemistry is the study of matter — what things are made of, what properties they have, and how they can change.

Matter

Everything around us that has mass and takes up space.

Atoms

Very small building blocks that make up matter.

Changes

Things can change from one form to another or form new substances.

Mixtures Around Us

A mixture is made when two or more substances are combined without a new substance forming. Choose an example to explore it.

Salt + Water

Salt dissolves in water and forms a solution.

Can it be separated? Yes — by evaporating the water, the salt is left behind.

Chemical Changes

A chemical change produces one or more new substances. Here are three safe, everyday examples.

Iron Rusting
Iron Rust

When iron meets water and oxygen over time, it slowly turns into a new, reddish-brown substance called rust.

Cooking Food
Raw egg Cooked egg

Heat changes the structure of the ingredients, so cooked food cannot easily turn back into its raw form.

Vinegar + Baking Soda
Vinegar + Soda Gas bubbles

Mixing them produces bubbles of carbon dioxide gas — a classic sign that a new substance has formed.
